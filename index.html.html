§ 01 · The Context

India's education infrastructure is at an inflection point.

The economics of standalone campuses are breaking. At the same time, new infrastructure models are emerging — vertical, shared, and standardised.

Pressures on the old model
01

Land cost has crossed the threshold where standalone campus economics break — operators are now under-writing for vertical and shared formats.

02

Capital intensity has decoupled from operator balance sheets — expansion is increasingly capex-light, lease-driven.

03

Demand for quality K-12 in Tier-1 + Tier-2 corridors is structurally outpacing supply — and the gap accelerates with every year of delayed format-shift, against NEP 2020's GER targets.

What's emerging in its place

New infrastructure formats are taking shape — not theoretical, but already being piloted by operators, developers, and capital.

Vertical urban campuses
Shared school districts
Scalable, standardised rollout formats

Education infrastructure is moving from isolated campuses to integrated systems built for scale, flexibility, and speed.

What we're seeing across the EduInfra Series room
OBSERVATION · 01

School groups expanding from 10–15 campuses to 50+ targets are running into the same wall — their existing capex stacks were never built for that scale. Asset-light infra partnerships are no longer optional.

OBSERVATION · 02

Multiple education-infrastructure capital vehicles have been formed since 2023 — each searching for the same scarce input: a defensible model to underwrite at scale.

OBSERVATION · 03

Developers with land suited to education density are now leading the conversation — surfacing parcels to find pre-qualified anchors before going to RFP. The old "operator finds land" dynamic has flipped.

§ 02 · Why This Matters Now

This transition is not theoretical. It is already underway.

Operators are piloting vertical formats. Developers are assembling district plays. Capital is searching for fundable frameworks. But the market is moving ahead of the conversation — and 2026 is when the lead authorship gets claimed.

What the market does not yet have
01A structured platform to evaluate these models — outside conference theatre.
02A forum where operators, developers, and capital align on the same table.
03A mechanism to convert ideas into real expansion decisions, on the record.
04A named author for the framework the next decade gets built against.

Why 2026 specifically. The first wave of vertical-format operators are at proof-of-model. New education-infra capital vehicles are being formed. Brownfield capacity unlock has become urgent for established groups. The frameworks defining the next decade are being written this year — and there is no neutral, credentialed room where they get tested. That gap is what this platform fills.
